How we farm

Organic here means what we leave out

The holding is certified under PGS-India at the Green tier — the in-conversion grade. Below is precisely what goes onto the soil and what never does, and you are welcome to come and see it for yourself.

Nothing synthetic

No synthetic fertiliser, no chemical pesticide, no herbicide, no GMO planting material. Soil is fed with farmyard manure, vermicompost and green mulch from the holding itself.

Water used carefully

Chitradurga is a dry district. Irrigation runs on drip lines to the tree base rather than flooding, so water reaches the root and not the weed.

Mixed, not monocropped

Coconut and areca overhead, pepper on the trunks, banana and cocoa below, flowers at the margins. Layers shade the soil, hold moisture, and keep pest pressure from ever building on one crop.

Reclaimed, not inherited

This land was dry and unproductive when the family took it on. Every bit of the present canopy was planted, irrigated and brought to bearing at the founder’s own cost.

On certification, plainly: the farm now holds a PGS-India scope certificate at the Green tier — the grade given to land part-way through conversion. That is not the same as PGS-India Organic, and we do not describe our produce as fully certified organic until the conversion period is complete. The certificate and its terms are set out in full below.

Who grows it

Dr. D. T. Subhash

The farm carries the name Rangadevathe, and the face on our seal is the elder in whose memory the holding is worked. Dr. D. T. Subhash, now seventy-one, took on land at Yelladakere that had gone dry and unproductive, and spent the better part of two decades putting it back into bearing — borewell and drip lines first, then the slow business of getting coconut and areca established on ground that had carried very little.

He funded the conversion himself, out of his own pocket, without a scheme or a subsidy behind it. The choice to farm it organically was made at the start rather than arrived at later: the soil had been depleted once already, and the point of the exercise was to rebuild it, not to extract another cycle out of it.

“Land that has been worked badly can be brought back. It only asks for patience and for you to stop poisoning it.”

In April 2026 the family placed the holding under a registered charitable trust so that it would outlast any one of them. The trust’s remit runs wider than the farm — organic and sustainable agriculture, horticultural research, religious observance for the family deities, and charitable work in food, medical aid and scholarships — but the farm is what pays for it. What you buy here funds that work directly.

Ordering

How an order actually works

We are a farm, not a warehouse. Orders are confirmed by a person, against what is actually ready to cut that week.

  1. Fill your basket

    Add quantities against the items you want. Rates shown are current farm-gate rates and can move with the season.

  2. Send it to us

    The basket becomes a message you send by WhatsApp, or read out over the phone. Nothing is charged at this point.

  3. We confirm availability

    We reply with what is ready, the final weight and rate, and the delivery or pickup date. Harvest happens after you confirm, not before.

  4. Pay on delivery

    Cash or UPI when the produce reaches you. Bulk and wholesale enquiries are welcome — tell us the quantity and we will quote.
